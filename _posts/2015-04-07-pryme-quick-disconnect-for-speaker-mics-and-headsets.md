<span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">If you are like me you've purchased a number of different handheld radios over the years. It's always nice to have some accessories for your radios, a speaker-mic or headset is a really nice addition when working events.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">Personally, I like to have a stealth-style headset. It allows me to hear the radio in a crowded environment and not bother the people around me. It also provides some privacy to the conversation.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">But, each time I purchase a radio it seems to have different connectors than my previous radio. I finally got tired of having to replace everything when I get a new radio.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">To make matters worse, I'm in a mode right now where I have multiple handheld radios from different manufacturers. I'm also finding that I'm like the manufacturer speaker-mics less and less over time. I want something I can hold on to, has a bigger speaker than the radio, and has a durable build.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">The Icom speaker-mic I purchased for my ID-51a is small and frail. To make matters worse, it has a small and weak speaker.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">I started looking for alternatives. I found [Pryme Radio Products](http://www.pryme.com/). Their target market is really commercial, law enforcement, and emergency services. But, they also have products for the amateur radio world.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">Using a readily available connector (Hirose), they have created a nice quick-disconnect system (QD). </span><span style="clear:right;float:right;font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;margin-bottom:1em;margin-left:1em;">![](http://1.bp.blogspot.com/-1CEme1A7alA/VSPrhfrtrwI/AAAAAAAANCc/TwJ6_kpjva8/s1600/qdfeature.jpg)</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">How does this all work? The basics are simple. You purchase the quick disconnect accessories you want. Then you purchase the quick disconnect adapter for your radio.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">Depending on the type of connectors on your radio you may have a cable or a module that connects to the side of your radio.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">In my case I have both. For my Icom ID-51a I have a cable like the upper image on the right. My Hytera PD782x (DMR) I have a module that is similar to the lower image on the right. Most of the modules are about $35.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">On the Hytera I can leave the module on all the time. When I remove the QD accessory the radio goes back to using the built-in speaker and microphone. I think this is a great feature.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">Now you understand the radio adapter. What kind of accessories can you get. </span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">In the image below you can see a sampling of the accessories available with the quick disconnect connector. I have the SPM-2205 "Trooper" speaker-mic, the SPM-3005 headset, and the SPM-1405 headset.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">The speaker-mic offers a loud speaker, great in the car, it also has a 3.5mm jack for connecting an earplug. On the back is a rotating spring clip. It's similar to what's on a lot of the radios now, making it easy to clip to clothing or a belt. I frequently clip it to the seat belt while I'm driving.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><div style="clear:both;text-align:center;">[<span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">![](http://1.bp.blogspot.com/-5isSOH8Fimo/VSPslOsG_VI/AAAAAAAANCk/3bn58v0PoAU/s1600/qdavailableontheseproducts.jpg)</span>](http://1.bp.blogspot.com/-5isSOH8Fimo/VSPslOsG_VI/AAAAAAAANCk/3bn58v0PoAU/s1600/qdavailableontheseproducts.jpg)</div><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">The SPM-3005 headset is nice. It's just the right length when I have the radio on my belt. There is a collar clip for the ear tube and the microphone and PTT are combined into a module that also has a clip. This makes for a nice rig when providing support for events.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">I purchased the SPM-1405 headset for base use. I use it when I'm doing nets from home. It is comfortable to wear, has a nice long cord and a big PTT part way down the cable.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">I am planning to get a Motorola DMR handheld radio soon. I'm happy know that I can use my existing accessories and not have to purchase new one.</span>
