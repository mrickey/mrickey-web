<span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">Most radios have time out timers, or TOT. They are there to protect the radio, and to protect resources like repeaters and links.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">A timeout timer will stop the radio from transmitting if the PTT is held down for too long. How long is too long? Well, that's up to each operator. I use 60 seconds on most of my radios.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">I recently started working with a Hytera PD782 DMR radio. This is a commercial radio and offers some extra features related to the time out timer.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">The think I want to highlight here is the pre-alarm for the TOT. This is an alarm you can have go off a few seconds before the TOT cuts off your transmission. I have mine set to 5 seconds.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">Take a look at the video for more details.</span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><div style="clear:both;text-align:center;"><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">\[youtube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kfLe--CZ9Vw?feature=player\_embedded\]</span></div><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;"></span><span style="font-family:Verdana, sans-serif;">Check back for more articles in the future.</span>
